The Challenge

Egyptian schools face a farming crisis

Despite owning cultivable land, most Egyptian schools misuse their agricultural potential — wasting water, relying on harmful pesticides, and leaving fertile soil unproductive.

Water Waste

Flood irrigation dominates school farms, consuming far more water than crops actually need and depleting local resources.

Pesticide Overuse

Without precision controls, students and staff are exposed to excessive pesticide applications that damage health and soil quality.

Mediterranean Fruit Fly

Invasive pest infestations destroy crops and lead to significant yield losses with no integrated management in place.

Underused Arable Land

School plots remain inefficiently cultivated, missing a key opportunity for hands-on STEM education and food production.

Our Answer

An autonomous microenvironment

Strawmato is a compact, fully self-regulating greenhouse system that maintains ideal growing conditions 24 h/day — using recycled components and zero internet dependency.

ESP32 Brain

Central microcontroller orchestrates all sensors and actuators via relay modules with event-driven logic.

DHT22 + Peltier Cooling

Temperature and humidity sensed continuously; Peltier modules + fans activate to bring temps from 35–42 °C ambient down to 15–20 °C.

Precision Water Pump

Soil moisture sensor triggers irrigation only when needed — delivering 19.6 N·m torque right to the root zone.

LDR-Controlled LED Strips

Light-dependent resistor monitors ambient light and activates grow LEDs to ensure consistent photosynthesis.

Control Logic

How it thinks

Every component is wired into a feedback loop. Sensors read real-world conditions; the ESP32 decides; actuators respond — all without human input.

STEP 01

Sense Temperature

DHT22 reads ambient temperature inside the enclosure every cycle.

STEP 02

Cool or Heat

If temp > 20 °C → Peltier + fans activate. If < 15 °C → resistive heaters engage.

STEP 03

Monitor Moisture

Soil moisture sensor detects dryness and triggers the water pump precisely.

STEP 04

Regulate Light

LDR monitors ambient light; LED strips switch on when intensity drops below threshold.

Why Strawmato

A better solution already existed — almost

Earlier approaches from Craig B. Tuttle's controlled-environment systems and Lufa Farms showed promise, but came with major trade-offs that made them impractical for Egyptian schools. Strawmato addresses each one.

Prior Solutions

  • Require stable internet connectivity
  • High energy consumption
  • Expensive commercial hardware
  • Not designed for low-resource school environments
  • Heavy water usage with no moisture feedback
  • Not replicable by students

Strawmato

  • Fully offline — zero internet dependency
  • Low-power design (runs 6 h/day)
  • Built from recycled / off-the-shelf components
  • Purpose-built for Egyptian STEM schools
  • Saves ~40% water via sensor-triggered irrigation
  • Student-replicable and curriculum-compatible

What's Next

Scaling to 100 m²

The next phase transforms the 0.25 m² proof-of-concept into a full-scale school greenhouse, powered by solar energy and managed by industrial-grade controllers.

Solar-Powered

Photovoltaic panels provide clean, off-grid energy to power the expanded greenhouse year-round.

Industrial Controllers

PLC-grade control systems replace ESP32 for robust, high-reliability management at scale.

Cooling Pads

Commercial evaporative cooling pads replace Peltier modules for efficient large-area temperature regulation.

Full 100 m²

From a desktop-sized prototype to a real walk-in greenhouse serving the entire school community.
